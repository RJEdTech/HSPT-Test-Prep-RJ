The test, section by section

Most of the benefit of preparing comes from not being surprised. Here is what each section actually asks for.

Before anything else

There is no penalty for a wrong answer. A blank and a wrong answer score exactly the same, so a guess is free. If you are down to two choices, guess. If you have ten seconds left and six questions untouched, fill them in. Leaving a question blank is the only way to guarantee you get nothing for it.

Six things that work in every section

  • Write on the test. Cross out choices you have ruled out. The booklet is yours.
  • Eliminate before you choose. Getting from four choices to two doubles your odds even if you end up guessing.
  • Easy first. Every question is worth the same. Answer what you know, then come back.
  • Skip and return rather than stalling. Thirty seconds spent stuck is two easier questions lost.
  • Trust your first read. Changing answers on a hunch loses more points than it gains.
  • Keep the answer sheet lined up. If you skip a question, skip its bubble. People lose real points to a one-row slip.

Verbal Skills — 60 questions, 16 minutes

Sixteen seconds a question. This section rewards knowing the word, not thinking hard about it. Five kinds of question appear, mixed together:

Synonyms

"A swift runner is:" — pick the closest word in meaning. If you do not know the word, look for pieces of it you do know. Prefixes and roots carry more than people expect.

Antonyms

Same skill, opposite direction — and the trap is that the test puts a synonym among the choices. When you are working fast it is easy to grab the word that feels related. Read the stem twice: opposite.

Analogies

"Red is to stop as green is to ___." Say the relationship out loud in a sentence before you look at the choices: red is the signal that means stop. Then test each option in that same sentence. Watch the part of speech — if the first pair is noun-to-noun, the answer is a noun.

Verbal logic

Three statements; if the first two are true, is the third true, false, or uncertain? This one has a rule, and it is worth learning. The third statement is false only if the first two make it impossible. If the first two simply do not settle the matter, the answer is uncertain — even when you know from the outside world that the statement is wrong.

"All fish can swim. Whales can swim. Whales are fish." You know whales are not fish. But nothing in the first two statements rules it out, so the answer is uncertain, not false. Students lose several points here every year by answering with what they know instead of what the statements prove.

Verbal classification

"Which word does not belong?" Find what three of them share, then the fourth is the odd one. If two different categories both seem to work, pick the more obvious one — the test is not trying to be clever.

Quantitative Skills — 52 questions, 30 minutes

Less about calculation than about seeing a relationship: number series, comparisons, and short reasoning problems. For a series, find the step between terms first, then check whether the step itself is changing. For a comparison, work out each quantity before you look at the answer choices.

Reading — 62 questions, 25 minutes

Two kinds of question: comprehension of passages, and vocabulary in context.

Read the questions first, then the passage. It tells you what you are reading for. Every answer is in the text — if you cannot point at the line that supports your choice, it is probably not the answer. The most common wrong answer is one that is true in the world but not stated in the passage.

For vocabulary in context, cover the choices, read the sentence with the word blanked out, and decide what would fit. Then look.

Mathematics — 64 questions, 45 minutes

Arithmetic, fractions, decimals, percents, basic geometry, simple equations, word problems. Study what you have already been taught — this section does not go beyond it.

Worth having cold before you walk in:

  • Integer signs: (−)(−) = +, (−)(+) = −, (+)(+) = +
  • Area = length × width · Volume = length × width × height · Perimeter = the sum of all sides
  • Percents: is / of = % / 100
  • The common fraction–decimal–percent equivalents: ½, ⅓, ¼, ⅕, ¾

No calculator. Estimate first — often the choices are far enough apart that a rough answer identifies the right one without full computation.

Language Skills — 60 questions, 25 minutes

Three sentences and a fourth option reading "No mistakes." Find the error in capitalization, punctuation, usage, or spelling — or decide there isn't one.

"No mistakes" is a real answer and it is correct more often than students expect. Do not talk yourself into finding an error that is not there. Read each sentence on its own rather than comparing them to each other, and check one thing at a time: capitals, then punctuation, then grammar.

Studying in the weeks before

Cramming the night before is the worst way to prepare and the most common. What works instead:

Short sessions, spread out

Twenty-five minutes of work, five-minute break, repeat. Four of those across a week beats one long session the night before — by a wide margin, in study after study. Spacing practice out is the single best-supported finding in the research on learning.

Test yourself instead of rereading

Rereading notes feels productive because the material starts to look familiar. Familiarity is not the same as being able to produce the answer. Cover the page and try to recall it, or use the practice sets — the effort of retrieving something is what makes it stick.

Sleep is part of studying

Memories consolidate during sleep. A late night before the test costs you more than the extra hour of review gains. Go to bed.

The morning itself

  • Sleep the night before. Eat breakfast. Both matter more than they sound like they do.
  • Arrive early enough that traffic is not part of your morning.
  • Bring two sharpened No. 2 pencils.
  • Wear layers — you cannot predict the room.
  • If a section goes badly, let it go. Each section is scored on its own, and carrying section two into section three is how one bad stretch becomes three.
