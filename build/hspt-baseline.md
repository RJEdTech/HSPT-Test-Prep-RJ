# HSPT Difficulty Baseline — derived from the licensed Peterson's diagnostic (298 items)

The real test, per form:
| Section | Q | Time | sec/Q |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verbal Skills | 60 | 16 min | 16 |
| Quantitative Skills | 52 | 30 min | 35 |
| Reading | 62 (40 comprehension + 22 vocabulary) | 25 min | 24 |
| Mathematics | 64 | 45 min | 42 |
| Language | 60 | 25 min | 25 |

Test-taker: 8th grader, ~13-14 years old. Scored on national percentile; the test must
discriminate across the whole range, so a bank where a strong 8th grader gets ~95% is
mis-leveled even if every item is "correct."

## Verbal Skills — Peterson's item mix and level
- **Analogies (10)**, form "X is to Y as A is to ___". Concrete relations
  (brown:dirt::blue:sky; stove:cook::bed:sleep; bouquet:vase::ice cube:tray;
  glass:window::wall:room). The RELATION is easy; the DISCRIMINATION comes from
  distractors that are semantically adjacent — for ice cube:tray the distractors are
  cold, bottle, water (all true of ice cubes, none is the container). Never a distractor
  that is simply absurd.
- **Verbal logic (12)**, form "…If the first two statements are true, the third is
  true / false / uncertain". Peterson's uses ONLY ordering and comparison relations
  (taller than, runs faster than, more expensive than, hungrier than, ate more than).
  Roughly balanced across true / false / uncertain, with "uncertain" arising from a
  broken chain (A>B, B<C, therefore A?C) and "false" from a contradicted ordering.
  Peterson's does NOT use categorical syllogisms ("All X are Y…") in this section.
- **Classification (16)**, "Which word does not belong with the others?" Traps that
  matter: category-vs-member (Dresser / Clothing / Suitcase / Wardrobe — three are
  storage, one is the thing stored); whole-vs-part (Handlebars / Seat / Bicycle /
  Wheels); part-of-speech or abstraction mismatch (Hopeful / Perspective / Positive /
  Optimistic); fine-grained subcategory (Cello / Violin / Guitar / Trumpet — strings vs
  brass, not "instrument vs non-instrument"); manner-of-X (Watch / Sing / Talk /
  Whisper). Never "three foods and a car."
- **Synonyms (13)**, phrase form "An artificial tree is ___". Tier: artificial, audible,
  elementary, absurd, pungent, gradual, hilarious, ignorant, jubilant, sinister,
  misplaced, lucid, humongous.
- **Antonyms (9)**, "Lavish means the opposite of ___". Tier: lavish, deteriorate,
  flair, squander, escalate, renounce, tumult.

## Quantitative Skills — four item types, no calculator
1. **Series (~20)**: whole numbers (6,10,14,18…), decreasing by growing steps
   (202,200,196,190,182…), letter series (QXR SXT UXV __ YXZ; CY DX EW __),
   alternating/interleaved (3,4,9,6,27,__,81,10 and 7,8,10,11,13,14,__),
   letter-count series (A, B, A, BB, A, BBB…), fraction series (3/4, 6/16, 12/64…),
   decimal series (1.2, 2.1, 1.8, 2.7, 2.4…), mixed alphanumeric (3D, E8, 3F, G8, __).
   The blank is not always last — "what number should come FIRST", "fill the middle".
2. **Geometric comparison (~10)**: figures A/B/C compared on area, length or angle
   measure; answer options are compound relations ("The area of A equals the area of C,
   and the area of B is greater than the area of A").
3. **Non-geometric comparison (~10)**: compare three expressions, e.g. A) 3^4 B) 4^3
   C) 3x4; A) 200% of 0.1, B) 50% of 1, C) 25% of 10; A) 0.03x10^3 B) 0.003x10^4
   C) 0.3x10^2. Answers are compound relations, not "A > B".
4. **Number manipulation (~12)**: "What number is 8 less than 3 cubed?", "Twice the sum
   of 4 and what number is the same as 4 times that number?", "One-third of what number
   leaves two less than 2/3 of that number?", "Three-fourths of what number is 3/4?"
   These are one- or two-step, phrased as convoluted English rather than as equations.
   THIS IS THE SIGNATURE HSPT ITEM TYPE and the one most often missing from prep banks.

## Mathematics — content ceiling actually tested
Place value and number names; expanded form with exponents; proportions; percent
(including percent decrease and two-stage percent); ratio; probability (single event,
complement); mean / median / mode; LCM / factors; order of operations; evaluating an
expression with negative and fractional values; one-step linear equations with decimals;
solving (b-2)(b+5)=0 given factored; inequalities with 0<x<1; measurement conversion
(metric and customary, and time arithmetic with borrowing); area of triangle,
parallelogram, composite figure, circle and semicircle perimeter; counting principle;
square-root estimation; word problems in money and rates.
NOT tested: systems of equations, graphing lines, slope, quadratic formula, functions,
trigonometry, radicals beyond estimation, standard deviation.

## Reading
- 4 passages, ~350-450 words: informational/expository (market research, sound waves),
  and one literary/historical excerpt in older prose (The Story of Napoleon).
- 10 questions per passage: main idea / best title; stated detail; vocabulary-in-context
  ("the word demographic in paragraph 2 most nearly means"); author's purpose for a
  specific example; inference about the author's attitude; prediction; comparison of two
  things in the passage; "which of the following is NOT mentioned"; analogy to something
  outside the passage ("the English Mercurie is like…").
- **22 separate vocabulary items** in phrase form: an articulate speaker, curtail the
  program, lucrative profession, great elation, a strong adversary, adjourn the meeting,
  effective technique, amend the rules, delete the text, a sentimental mood, a cordial
  encounter, a sluggish movement, an erroneous explanation, to withhold information,
  a frivolous purchase, an oppressive government, a valiant decision, to eliminate waste,
  a lamentable ending, a slovenly roommate, a wayward kid, a vibrant design.

## Language
- **~40 error-identification items**: three sentences A/B/C plus D "(No mistakes)".
  Punctuation, capitalization and usage are MIXED, not segregated. The two non-error
  sentences are fully correct and unremarkable; the error is a single specific fault
  (pedestrian's for pedestrians, run-on, "her's", "I would of called", "The trees leaves",
  semicolon where a colon belongs, "president John F. Kennedy", song title unitalicized/
  unquoted, "Neither Grace or Yuri").
- **~10 spelling items**, same A/B/C/(No mistakes) shape, one misspelling per item:
  concieve, sissors, arguement, acheive, Febuary, cantalope, noticeable(correct),
  mischievous(correct), embarrassing(correct).
- **~10 composition items**: "Choose the sentence that demonstrates correct usage";
  "Choose the best word to join the thoughts together"; "Choose the word that best
  completes this sentence"; "Which of these expresses the idea most clearly?";
  "Which sentence does not belong in the paragraph?" (numbered 1-4); "Choose the pair of
  sentences that best develops this topic sentence"; "Which sentence offers the LEAST
  support to the topic…".
- "(No mistakes)" is the key on roughly 15-25% of error-identification items — often
  enough that a student cannot safely rule it out.
