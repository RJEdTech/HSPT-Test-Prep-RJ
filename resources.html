More places to practice

The practice sets on this site are a start, not a library. If you want more — especially in a section you keep missing — here is where else to look.

Please read this first. The sites below are not run by Regis Jesuit, and we have not reviewed, tested or approved their content, their accuracy or their pricing. We list them only because families ask where else to practice and we would rather point you somewhere than leave you guessing. We do not vet their quality. Some are free, some will try to sell you something, and some may have changed since we last looked. Use your own judgment, and check with a parent or guardian before signing up for anything or paying for anything.

The test publisher

The HSPT is written and scored by Scholastic Testing Service. They are the authority on anything official — what the test contains, how it is scored, accommodations and score reports.

  • Scholastic Testing Service — the company behind the HSPT, including a parents' page with sample score reports.
  • STS HSPT questions and answers — their FAQ on registration, test dates, accommodations, retesting and scores.

For anything specific to testing at Regis Jesuit — dates, registration, accommodations, fee waivers — contact the Regis Jesuit Admissions Office rather than STS.

Full-length HSPT practice

Sites offering practice questions across all five sections. Quality varies, and several use free questions as a route into a paid product.

  • Mometrix HSPT Academy — free practice questions with worked explanations, alongside paid materials.
  • Test Prep Review — free question sets organized by section.
  • Study Guide Zone — a free HSPT study guide and practice questions, including a downloadable PDF.
  • PracticeQuiz — free question banks for high school placement tests.
  • Peterson's — topic lessons and online practice tests. Paid, with a free trial period.

Verbal Skills — vocabulary and word relationships

The fastest way to improve here is simply to know more words. That is a habit, not a cram — fifteen minutes a day for a month beats four hours the weekend before.

  • Vocabulary.com — adaptive vocabulary practice that adjusts to what you already know.
  • Quizlet — search it for "HSPT" and you will find flashcard sets made by other students and teachers. Quality is uneven; look at who made a set before trusting it.
  • Khan Academy — 8th grade reading and vocabulary — free, with vocabulary work built around real passages.

Reading

Reading comprehension improves mostly by reading — but reading with questions in front of you builds the specific skill this section tests.

  • Khan Academy — 8th grade reading — free passages with comprehension questions.
  • CommonLit — a free library of reading passages with questions, searchable by grade level.
  • Newsela — news articles rewritten at several reading levels, with questions. Free and paid tiers.

Language Skills — grammar, punctuation, spelling

  • Khan Academy — 7th and 8th grade grammar — free, with more than 60 exercises on parts of speech, subject–verb agreement and sentence structure.
  • The Blue Book of Grammar and Punctuation — clear rule explanations with free quizzes, useful when you know you got something wrong but not why.

Mathematics and Quantitative Skills

Both math sections stay within what you have already been taught. If a topic feels shaky, work the topic itself rather than more HSPT questions about it.

  • Khan Academy — Grade 8 math — free lessons and practice, with video explanations for every topic.
  • Khan Academy — Pre-algebra — fractions, decimals, percents, ratios: the ground most HSPT math questions stand on.
  • IXL — Grade 8 math — skill-by-skill practice. A limited number of free questions per day; paid beyond that.

A word about paid tutoring

You do not need to pay anyone to prepare for the HSPT. Plenty of students do well on it having used nothing but free material and their own schoolwork. If your family is considering paid tutoring or a prep course, that is your decision to make. Please do not read it as something Regis Jesuit expects or requires, because it is not.
